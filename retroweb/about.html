Help / About

Vintage machines, running entirely in your browser.

This site contains era-correct microcomputer and arcade emulators simulating hardware from the 1970s through the 1990s. The goal here is to emulate the physical hardware as accurately as possible. This includes full CPU instruction sets, clock speeds, bus architecture, peripheral boards and ICs.

How the site works

The hardware emulation is written from scratch in C++ and compiled to a WebAssembly module so the system runs natively in your browser. There are no virtual machines or 3rd party emulator engines involved. Where applicable, hard drive and ROM images are automatically saved/updated in the browser's IndexedDB storage so you don't lose your data. Note that none of your ROMs, floppy disks, hard disks or paper tapes are uploaded to our servers. Everything is stored only in your browser or your Google Drive account.

High-Scores: For arcade games with high-scores, the emulators will store your scores in browser storage alongside your ROMs to maintain them between sessions. The games are true to life where some only ran a single high-score (no initials) and some store ranked initials. What may not be true to life is traditionally high-scores reset when the game lost power. Since we are in a browser, the games "lose power" all the time so storing off high-scores makes it more fun. You can reset your high-scores at any time.

Google Drive

Some emulators provide integration with Google Drive which is currently by invitation only. Please contact me below if you'd like to use this feature.

Why is software and game ROMs not included?

This site generally doesn't host proprietary software. However, it does provide a way for you to add such software or ROMs to the emulators. Systems featuring ROMs, hard drives, floppy drives or tape readers allow you to install and run software like you would have back in the day. Everything is avaialbe on the web. I would not have built an emulator if I couldn't easily find the software and game ROMs online.

Use of AI

The work for this site is done in conjunction with Artificial Intelligence (AI), in particular, Claude and Cursor. I am an old-school professional programmer but I found that AI could allow me to build complex hardware emulation without poring over dense specifications. So, I decided to take on the role of a software architect where I prompted the AI agents to build the software how I envisioned.

I know there are AI haters out there (I used to be one) but I invite you to check out the site anyway. I've done my best to assure no AI-slop so any bugs should be traditional in nature. If AI is really not your jam, thats fine. There are plenty of non-AI built emulators out there.

Why I Chose the Emulators

The emulators built on this site are primarily chosen from my own experience or curiosity:

  • Altair 8800: This one fascinates me for its bare-bones raw programming. Not to mention, it was one of the first microcomputers.
  • IBM PC-AT: This was my first computer. I wrote so many BASIC programs on it.
  • 486 DX-66: My first custom built PC (Computer Shopper!). However, I don't remember the exact hardware I used so this implementation is era correct maxed out.
  • 6502 Assembler: I tried to build this IRL but failed. This is loosly based on the Ben Eater board.
  • Arcades: These are just cool and fairly simple since the boards have only one purpose. You may notice arcades grouped by CPU. Once you emulate a game, the related games are (mostly) easy. Also notice in the descriptions how some original arcades ran two or more CPUs. Those are failthully emulated here as they were built back in the day.

What's up with the themes?

Since most of the systems emulated here pre-date the web, I just went with 1990s themes to keep them retro web rather than 1970s or 1980s hardware. The modern themes are really so I could offer light and dark modes with full width pages. I did let AI design a theme all on its own. It did alright, though it basically just copied the Windows 95 theme. I'm not a UX/UI designer so if you have ideas for improvements or other themes, contact me below.
