Help / About

How the site works

This site emulates retro computer and game systems running entirely in your browser. The emulators are written from scratch in C++ and compiled to a WebAssembly module. The module is downloaded to the browser, allowing the emulator to execute all hardware simulation locally. There are no virtual machines or third-party emulator engines involved.

Where applicable, hard drive and ROM images are automatically saved in the browser's IndexedDB storage so you don't lose your data. Also, none of your ROMs, floppy disks, hard disks or cassette/paper tapes are uploaded to our servers. So when you load removable media, the images stay local to your browser.

Removable Media Formats: Media such as floppy disks, CDs, cassette/paper tapes and ROMs must be loaded from raw binary dumps. Each emulator will have instructions on which types of files are supported. If you have physical media, you will need to convert them to the expected binary dumps. This site does not support physical devices connected through USB or other means.

Hard Drives: Hard disk images are stored in your browser's IndexedDB and are compressed to save space. The drives are synced to the browser storage in near real-time so you don't have to worry about losing your data. However, it's recommended that you download your hard drive images periodically as a backup.

High-Scores: For arcade games with high-scores, the emulators will store your scores in browser storage so they persist across sessions. You can reset your high-scores at any time.

Why are some software titles and game ROMs not included?

This site does not host commercial software or game ROMs where active rights holders still enforce their copyrights. Some titles like Altair BASIC and CP/M have long since been redistributed among the retro community and therefore are included. If you are an active rights holder of such software and would like it removed, please contact me below.

Arcade ROMs: The game ROMs for the arcades are not available on this site. Instead, each arcade starts with a hardware test ROM. It's up to you to obtain the actual ROM and load it into the emulator. As noted earlier, the ROM will be saved locally in your browser's IndexedDB so it will load the game thereafter.

Browser Support

Chrome or Edge are recommended as most testing is done in Chrome, however, the site should run fine in all major browsers. If you use privacy extensions or privacy-focused browsers, you may encounter issues with sound, fullscreen or browser storage.

Found a bug?

Even though the systems are relatively simple, they still require extensive testing. If you run into any bugs or issues, please reach out below. Also, I could use your help testing! Contact me if you'd like to get involved. Any help is much appreciated.

About the Creator

Hello, I'm Chris, the creator of this site. I've been a professional software engineer for over 25 years and a computer geek for much longer than that. I was lucky enough to experience mid-to-late retro computing and gaming before it was retro.

My Dad bought a Commodore 64 around 1983 or 1984 and I was able to convince him to let me get one game for it. It was also my first experience with the BASIC programming language, though I was too young to do much with it.

The first memorable computer for me, which my Dad also bought, was the IBM PC/AT (emulated here). I spent hours and hours programming BASIC on it and he even let me move it to my room at some point. It was so exciting to have my "own" computer.

My favorite rig was my 486 (also emulated here) which I sourced from Computer Shopper, the computer bible of the 1990s. With that one I learned C and C++, and played many hours of Doom on it. My friend and I even learned how to "network" two 486s to play multiplayer Doom. So much fun.

I also love the classic arcades. I remember the days as a kid in the 1980s getting a handful of quarters and playing the original games in the arcades. The sounds alone where so cool. As an adult, I built my own stand-up MAME cabinet. Though its been more fun creating the arcade emulators here to learn exactly how they worked.

By the way, my last name starts with "G" so "RetroCG" is either "Retro Computers & Games" or "Retro computers & games by Chris G." or just "Retro Chris G." (I am kind of old), whichever you prefer.
