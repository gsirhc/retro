Help / About

This site emulates retro computer and game systems running entirely in your browser. The emulator cores are written from scratch in C++ and compiled to a WebAssembly module. The module is downloaded to the browser, allowing the emulator to execute all hardware simulation locally. There are no virtual machines or third-party emulator engines involved.

Removable Media Formats

Media such as floppy disks, CDs, cassette/paper tapes and ROMs must be loaded from raw binary dumps. Each emulator will have instructions on which types of files are supported. If you have physical media, you will need to convert them to the expected binary dumps. This site does not support physical devices connected through USB or other means.

Hard Drives

Hard disk images are stored in your browser's IndexedDB and changes are saved every few seconds so you don't have to worry about losing your data. To be sure your hard drive is synced, power off the machine before navigating away from the page. It's also recommended that you download your hard drive images periodically as a backup.

High-Scores

For arcade games with high-scores, the emulators will store your scores in browser storage so they persist across sessions. You can reset your high-scores at any time.

Why are some software titles and game ROMs not included?

This site does not host commercial software or game ROMs where active rights holders still enforce their copyrights. Some titles like Altair BASIC and CP/M have long since been redistributed among the retro community and therefore are included. If you are an active rights holder of such software and would like it removed, please contact me below.

Browser Support

Chromium-based browsers are recommended as most testing is done in Chrome. However, the site should run fine in all major browsers. If you use privacy extensions or privacy-focused browsers, you may encounter issues with sound, fullscreen or browser storage.

System Requirements

The emulators should run on any modern desktop, laptop, tablet or even smartphone. The heavier emulators, such as the 486, may suffer performance issues on lower-end or older devices. Since the emulators run in the browser, you should ensure you have around 1GB of available RAM after the browser is loaded.

About the Creator

Hello, I'm Chris, the creator of this site. I've been a professional software engineer for over 25 years and a computer geek for much longer than that.

My Dad bought a Commodore 64 around 1983 or 1984 which was my first taste of home computing. I remember it was connected to a black and white TV and it also introduced me to the BASIC programming language.

The first really memorable computer for me, which my Dad also bought, was the IBM PC/AT (emulated here). I spent hours and hours programming BASIC on it and he even let me move it to my bedroom at some point. It was so exciting to have my "own" computer.

My favorite rig was a 486 (also emulated here) which I sourced from Computer Shopper, the computer bible of the 1990s. With that one I learned C and C++, and played many hours of Doom on it. My friend and I even learned how to "network" two 486s to play multiplayer Doom. So much fun.

I also love the classic arcades. I remember the days as a kid in the 1980s getting a handful of quarters and playing the original games in the arcades. The sounds alone were so cool. As an adult, I built my own stand-up MAME cabinet.

By the way, my last name starts with "G" so "RetroCG" is either "Retro Computers & Games" or "Retro computers & games by Chris G." or just "Retro Chris G." (I am kind of old), whichever you prefer.
