About

Vintage machines, running entirely in your browser.

This site contains era-correct microcomputer and arcade emulators simulating hardware from the 1970s through the 1990s. The goal here is to emulate the physical hardware as accurately as possible. This includes full CPU instruction sets, clock speeds, bus architecture, peripheral boards and ICs.

How the site works

The emulators are written in C++ and built into WebAssembly (WASM) files. Where applicable, hard drive and ROM images are automatically saved/updated in the browser's IndexedDB storage so you don't lose your data when you leave the site.

Google Drive

Some emulators provide integration with Google Drive which is currently by invitation only. Please contact me below if you'd like to use this feature. Send the email from your Google Drive account, or include which email you'd like me to use.

Why is software not included?

This site generally doesn't host proprietary software. However, it does provide a way for you to add such software to the emulators. Emulators with ROMs, hard drives, floppy drives or tape readers allow you to install or run software like you would have back in the day.

Use of AI

The majority of the work was done using Artificial Intelligence (AI) agents, in particular, Claude and Cursor. I am an old-school professional programmer but I found that AI could allow me to build complex hardware emulation without poring over dense specifications. I decided to take on the role of a software architect where I prompted the AI agents to build the software how I envisioned.

Why I Chose the Emulators

The emulators built on this site are primarily chosen from my own experience or curiosity:

  • Altair 8800: This one fascinates me for its raw programming. I was born when it came out.
  • IBM PC-AT: This was my first computer. I wrote so many BASIC programs on it.
  • 486 DX-66: My first custom built PC (Computer Shopper!). I learned C++ and played so much Doom.
  • 6502 Programmer: I tried to build this IRL but failed. I've been having fun learning assembly.
  • Arcades: I built a full-sized MAME arcade many years ago. It's fun to learn the original hardware.

What's Next?

I have too many ideas so there is much more to come. The 486 may be the most "modern" system I build as it's very complex due to the paged and protected memory. To emulate Pentiums or newer will be difficult.
